# 00 — Master Brief

## 1. Product vision

Naman Kitchen is an Indian modular kitchen company focused primarily on customers in Indore and Madhya Pradesh, with an ambition to grow across India through customers, dealers and franchise partners.

The website must communicate:

> **A smart design for every home.**

The emotional proposition is:

> **A kitchen that looks premium, works intelligently, fits your home, and is backed by a partner you can trust.**

The site should make the visitor feel:
- "They understand Indian homes."
- "Their designs look premium."
- "They care about practical storage."
- "I can trust them with my home."
- "Getting started looks easy."
- "I should request a consultation."

## 4. Audience

### Primary: Homeowners

Especially:
- New-home buyers.
- Apartment owners.
- Families renovating a kitchen.
- Couples designing a first home.
- Customers in Indore and nearby Madhya Pradesh markets.

They care about:
- Price/value.
- Durability.
- Storage.
- Looks.
- Ease of cleaning.
- Space utilization.
- Installation.
- Trust.

### Secondary: Architects/interior designers

They need:
- Design confidence.
- Reliable execution.
- Professional coordination.
- Hardware/material options.
- Consistent quality.

### Business audience

Dealers and franchise prospects need:
- Clear brand story.
- Business opportunity.
- Process transparency.
- Contact pathway.

Do not make the homepage feel like a franchise recruitment site. Homeowners must remain the dominant audience.

## 6. Creative rule

Every visual design decision should answer one of these questions:

1. Does it improve clarity?
2. Does it communicate craftsmanship?
3. Does it make the kitchen feel tangible?
4. Does it improve trust?
5. Does it help the user move toward consultation?

If not, remove it.

## 7. "Kitchen as interface" concept

Use the kitchen itself as the design system.

Examples:
- Section dividers can resemble cabinet seams.
- Cards can have inset edges like cabinet doors.
- Hover lines can behave like drawer tracks.
- Image masks can echo countertop corners.
- Long shadows can resemble cabinet overhangs.
- Horizontal rules can look like handle rails.
- Small labels can look like manufacturing tags.
- Measurements can appear as subtle architectural annotations.
- The navigation can feel like a slim kitchen hardware rail.
- Product filters can resemble finish/material sample chips.
- A consultation form can feel like a kitchen design worksheet.
- The footer can be composed like a final kitchen elevation drawing.

Keep these metaphors subtle. The site should still feel modern and easy to use.
